Living heritage

Cultural knowledge shared through practice.

Traditions are expressed through clothing, weaving, arts, crafts, and other practices that communities continue and pass on. In Zamboanga, these expressions reflect creativity, shared knowledge, and the identities of the city's diverse communities. Each tradition has its own meaning and deserves to be understood and appreciated with respect.

Made by tradition

Craft, identity, and cultural expression.

Explore some of the traditional practices and creative expressions connected with Zamboanga's cultural heritage.

01 / CLOTHING

Traditional Clothing

Traditional garments are expressions of cultural identity and creative skill. Clothing styles and meanings vary among communities; learning about them helps visitors appreciate the knowledge carried through their designs and making.

02 / TEXTILES

Yakan Weaving

Yakan tennun textiles are recognized for their colorful designs and careful craftsmanship. Weaving carries creative knowledge and skills that are practiced and shared across generations.

03 / MARITIME HERITAGE

Vinta and Its Cultural Significance

The vinta is a traditional wooden boat known for its colorful sails, which feature geometric patterns and vibrant colors. It is closely associated with the Sama, Badjao, and other maritime communities of Mindanao.

Historically, vintas were used for fishing, transportation, and travel between islands. Today, they represent Zamboanga's maritime heritage and cultural identity. The colorful vinta is also a recognizable symbol of the city and is prominently featured during the Regatta de Zamboanga.

04 / CRAFTS

Traditional Arts and Crafts

Textiles, Subanen baskets, and vinta-inspired artwork show how materials and creative skills become meaningful cultural expressions. These crafts reflect the knowledge and creativity of the communities who make them.

05 / MUSIC

Traditional Music

Zamboanga City's traditional music reflects its diverse cultural heritage, particularly the Chavacano, Sama-Bangingi, Yakan, and Subanen communities. Singing and indigenous musical instruments express cultural identity, history, celebrations, and community life.

Kulintang

A set of small horizontal gongs arranged in a row. It produces melodic patterns and is used in traditional musical performances.

06 / DANCE

Chavacano Cultural Dance

Traditional music and dances reflect the diverse cultural heritage of the Chavacano, Sama-Bangingi, Yakan, and Subanen communities. Rhythmic movements and colorful traditional clothing express cultural identity, history, celebrations, and community life.

Chavacano de Zamboanga is a lively cultural dance that portrays the social life, customs, and romantic courtship of early Zamboangueños. It represents couples enjoying leisurely walks along boulevards, parks, and waterfront areas while expressing admiration and affection through graceful dance movements.

The performance combines elegant partner steps with Spanish-influenced footwork called zapateados, which involves rhythmic stamping of the feet. Female dancers traditionally wear the mascota, a dress featuring butterfly sleeves and a flowing skirt, while male dancers wear formal suits. Accessories such as the paypay (fan) and sombrero (hat) add character to the performance.

More than entertainment, this dance reflects the Spanish influence on Zamboanga's performing arts and preserves aspects of traditional Zamboangueño clothing, music, courtship, and community life. It serves as a cultural expression of the city's heritage during festivals and other cultural celebrations.

Music • City identity • Heritage

Zamboanga Hermosa

“Beautiful Zamboanga.”

Unlike No Te Vayas de Zamboanga, Zamboanga Hermosa is officially recognized as the city's hymn.

Lyrics
Vicente M. Orendain
Music
Borromeo Lou
Musical arrangement
Miguel Velarde Jr.
Official recognition
City Ordinance No. 396, Series of 2012

The song was written to express the beauty of Zamboanga City, its beautiful women, its culture, and its importance as the pride of Mindanao. According to the ordinance, Vicente Orendain was a Zamboangueño musician, poet, orator, and lyricist who dedicated the song to Zamboanga City.

The city government requires public and private schools, universities, government offices, and other institutions to sing the hymn after the Philippine National Anthem during appropriate flag ceremonies and patriotic programs.

Who sings the song?

The official musical score identifies its composer, lyricist, and arranger, but it does not establish one original or exclusive singer. It is performed collectively by students, government employees, and other members of the community during official ceremonies.

Why No Te Vayas de Zamboanga and Zamboanga Hermosa matter

  • Preserve culture and history by keeping the stories and musical traditions of earlier generations alive.
  • Express cultural identity through the city's Spanish-influenced and Chavacano heritage.
  • Remind residents and visitors of the city's beauty and unique identity.
  • Bring Zamboangueños together and strengthen their sense of belonging.
  • Help pass traditions to younger generations, encouraging students to appreciate the city's history, language, and cultural heritage.
